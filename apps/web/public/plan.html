The Plan

Help shouldn't be hard. Anywhere.

FEED. starts in Vermont — where the need is real and the community is ready. Then it grows, state by state, until finding help is as simple as a library card. Here's how.

It starts in Vermont

The need is here — right now.

Federal programs that Vermonters depend on are being cut or eliminated. FEED. helps people find what's still available and apply without getting lost.

Less overhead, more food

Every dollar should reach people, not paperwork.

SNAP already delivers about 93–94 cents of every dollar directly to food. That's one of the most efficient programs in government. But even then, people miss out — not because they don't qualify, but because finding, applying, and tracking benefits takes time, paperwork, and know-how that not everyone has.

FEED. removes the navigation overhead. It helps people find programs they qualify for, fills in what it already knows about them, and tracks where every application stands. That means community money and energy go to food, care, and connection — not to getting lost in the system.

To be clear: FEED. doesn't hand out benefits. It helps people find and apply for the programs that already exist — and makes sure fewer fall through the cracks.

From Vermont to all 50 states

An open system — not a central empire.

FEED. is a Vermont nonprofit in formation. It runs here first. The open system is built so that people in other states can stand up their own community's FEED. The founder provides the system — not the infrastructure for all.

Each state runs its own instance. No single company controls it. FEED. is never for sale and never for profit. That's what keeps it free forever.

The vision: community access points in every state — free kiosks where anyone can search for help, even 3D-printed in the shape of wheat or a sunflower, so finding help is as normal as walking into a library.
